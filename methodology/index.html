The distinction

DelQuant is not trying to be the biggest data company in the room.

Large providers are built to deliver breadth: scores, schedules, odds, injuries, lineups, and feeds across many leagues. DelQuant is built to make one NBA intelligence layer more useful, more interpretable, and easier to test.

What we are actually selling

Not another number. A point of view you can inspect.

A standard projection answers “what is the number?” DelQuant is designed to answer the next questions: why this number, how stable is it, what changed recently, what historical players looked similar, and what decision does the output support?

The model stack

Several layers, one coherent output.

1. Structural baseline

Weighted multi-season performance, cohort shrinkage for sparse samples, recent-season variance, age profiles, usage sanity checks, and durability expectations establish the player’s underlying projection.

2. Responsive adjustment

Explicit horizon modes allow the structural view to be compared with recent-window views and game-day context. The response preserves both the structural projection and the adjusted projection.

3. Risk and confidence

Uncertainty is part of the product. DelQuant exposes range, volatility, availability, and confidence so downstream users can distinguish a strong forecast from a fragile one.

4. Player shape

Archetypes, competitive traits, historical comparisons, and capability flags describe how the player produces and what kinds of roster or workflow questions the output can answer.

5. Decision translation

The same intelligence can feed player cards, historical research, lineup tools, roster construction, market analysis, reports, and customer-facing applications.

6. Delivery

Outputs are available through API, JSON, batch, and report-ready surfaces. The integration can begin with one endpoint and expand only when the use case proves it deserves more.

Statistical methods

Transparent techniques, applied in layers.

The first DelQuant model is deterministic and structured. It is not presented as a mysterious neural forecast. Each layer has a job, a data source, and a way to inspect the result.

Weighted baseline

Recent seasons, not one hot year

For a player with enough history, the baseline weights the most recent season at 50%, the prior season at 30%, and the season before that at 20%. If fewer seasons are available, the weights are renormalized.

baseline = 0.50 × recent season + 0.30 × prior season + 0.20 × two seasons back
Shrinkage

Stabilize thin samples

Young players, role players, and small samples can produce extreme estimates. DelQuant blends player observations toward an age × position × role cohort so the estimate becomes more stable without erasing the player’s own signal.

shrunk estimate = player signal + cohort prior weighted by sample strength
Age and role

Development is part of the prior

The baseline is blended with an age, position, and role profile. The current season layer uses a 70% player baseline and 30% age-profile blend, with usage and minutes sanity checks.

projection = 0.70 × player baseline + 0.30 × age / role profile

Uncertainty and risk

The range matters as much as the mean.

Context-aware variance

DelQuant does not rely on one flat volatility number. It can examine trailing 20-, 40-, and 82-game windows, recency-weighted variance, home/away splits, rest and back-to-back splits, minutes bands, and opponent tiers.

The recency layer uses an exponential decay with a 10-game half-life, so meaningful role or usage changes can appear faster than they would in a season-only average.

Non-parametric uncertainty

Where a variance estimate needs an interval, the engine uses bootstrap resampling: repeatedly resample the relevant games, recompute the standard deviation, and report the 5th and 95th percentiles as a 90% interval.

bootstrap_std_dev_ci_90 = [5th percentile, 95th percentile]

This avoids assuming that every player-stat distribution is normally shaped.

Player-shape statistics

From box-score shape to basketball role.

54 offensive families

Offensive player shapes are mapped into position-specific clusters using scaled distance across points, rebounds, assists, steals, blocks, and made threes. The output includes the assigned family, distance from its centroid, and cluster confidence.

31 defensive families

The defensive taxonomy adds a separate role layer for rim protection, pressure, possession control, matchup responsibility, and defensive impact. Offense and defense remain distinct signals until the decision requires them to be combined.

Archetype-prior variance

A player’s observed variance can be blended toward the variance of the historical archetype. The current prior strength is 20 games, giving thin player samples a relevant role-based comparison.

shrunk variance = (n × player variance + 20 × archetype variance) / (n + 20)

Historical comparisons

The 31-season archive supplies player-season examples for comp searches, archetype priors, rarity analysis, and career-shape context. A comp is evidence of similar statistical shape—not a claim that two players are identical.

Projection horizons

One season view is not enough for every decision.

A roster decision, a current-form question, and a game-day workflow should not be forced through the same weighting scheme.

Structural

Season baseline

Use the multi-season baseline for stable valuation, long-run player evaluation, and rest-of-season planning.

Responsive

Recent windows

Use 10-game, 5-game, or 1-game recency views when current role, usage, or form should have more influence.

Situational

Game day

Apply schedule, rest, location, matchup, and slate context when the question is about today rather than the season.

Historical intelligence

The differentiator is the explanation layer.

Comparable players

Find historical player-seasons with similar production and role shape. Use the comparison to create context, not to pretend that two careers are identical.

Archetypes

Map player production into repeatable offensive, defensive, and roster-construction patterns that can be used across players and eras.

Traits and fit

Use Motor, Decision, and Pressure signals with player profiles to frame questions about role, fit, and team-building—not just box-score output.

Validation

Test the model before you build around it.

DelQuant supports dated historical replay with no-lookahead behavior. Freeze the player pool, date range, and scoring rules, then compare DelQuant with the current provider or a simple baseline.

What gets measured

  • Forecast accuracy by horizon and category
  • Calibration of confidence and uncertainty
  • Stability when the recent window changes
  • Incremental value over the current source
  • Integration effort and response reliability
